# Goods handed over at the counter are supplied in the Business's state

An Invoice's Place of supply is the Business's own state unless the sale is
marked as delivered, in which case it is the state of the address of delivery.
This holds whoever the Customer is, including a Registered customer whose
Address on record is in another state and who takes the goods away over the
counter. That sale is taxed as CGST plus SGST.

## Considered Options

Taking the Place of supply from the Customer's Address on record is what the
glossary first said, and it is simpler, because nothing on the sale has to be
asked. It is wrong for a counter. Under s.10(1)(a) of the IGST Act, the place
of supply of goods is where their movement ends for delivery to the
recipient. For goods collected at the shop, that is the shop. Charging IGST to
every out-of-state customer who walks in would misstate tax on the most common
kind of sale this screen makes. The contested case is a Registered customer who
carries the goods to their own state themselves. Some read the movement as
ending there. billow takes the counter reading and makes the delivered case
explicit rather than inferred.

## Consequences

The Customer's state no longer decides tax at all unless the Operator says the
goods are being delivered. A Business that disagrees with the counter reading
for out-of-state Registered customers marks those sales as delivered. Changing
the default later would not change Invoices already issued, because each one
keeps its own Place of supply.
